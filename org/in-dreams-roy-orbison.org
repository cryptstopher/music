** Tips

- LH plays root note in bass; RH plays full chord
- Fm adds dark color — the Ab is the key note (black key, one semitone below A)
- G7 resolves strongly to C; lean into that pull
- D7 is a secondary dominant — it pulls toward G/G7

* Advanced Techniques

** Inversions

Inversions keep the LH bass moving smoothly instead of jumping around.  Use these as voice-leading substitutes for root-position chords.

| Chord | Inversion      | Notes (low → high) | When to Use                        |
|-------+----------------+--------------------+------------------------------------|
| C/E   | C first inv.   | E – G – C          | Walking bass up to F               |
| C/G   | C second inv.  | G – C – E          | Pedal-tone feel; C over G bass     |
| F/A   | F first inv.   | A – C – F          | Smooth voice leading from Am       |
| F/C   | F second inv.  | C – F – A          | Approaching G7 from above          |
| G7/B  | G7 first inv.  | B – D – F – G      | Connects C → G7 with stepwise bass |
| G7/D  | G7 second inv. | D – F – G – B      | Floatier, less resolved feel       |
| Dm/F  | Dm first inv.  | F – A – D          | Smooth descent from F chord        |


Voice leading tip:

In the verse progression C → Dm → G7 → C, try this bass line:

- C (root) → D (root) → G (root) → C (root)
- Or the smoother: C → F (Dm/F) → G → E (C/E)

** Extended & Substitution Chords

These add color without changing the harmonic function.

   | Original | Extended Version | Added Notes | Character                       |
   |----------+------------------+-------------+---------------------------------|
   | G7       | G9               | + A above F | Warmer, more open dominant      |
   | G7       | G13              | + E above A | Full jazz dominant; very lush   |
   | C        | Cmaj7            | + B natural | Dreamy, slightly suspended      |
   | C        | C6               | + A         | Vintage feel; common in 50s/60s |
   | Dm       | Dm9              | + E above C | Richer minor color              |
   | Am       | Am7              | + G natural | Softer; blurs the Am → Dm move  |
   | F        | Fmaj7            | + E natural | Adds shimmer to the F chord     |

*** G13 Voicing for Piano (RH only)
   
       #+BEGIN_EXAMPLE
       LH: G (bass)
       RH: B – F – A – E   (3rd, 7th, 13th, stacked from bottom)
       #+END_EXAMPLE
       Drop the D and G from RH entirely — the ear fills them in from context.

*** Cmaj7 as an Opener

Try starting Verse 1 on Cmaj7 instead of plain C: C – E – G – B

It gives the opening that wistful, half-awake quality that suits the lyric.

** Rhythmic/Textural Ideas

- *Arpeggiate the chords* (broken chord, LH): root → 5th → octave while RH holds the triad
- *Ballad pattern* (LH): root on beat 1, chord on beats 2 and 3 (waltz-feel variation)
- *Sustained pedal tone*: hold G in the bass through the Bridge (Fm sections) for a suspended, unresolved tension that releases when C returns
- *Bridge dynamics*: drop to pp on "But just before the dawn," swell into the Fm, then pull back again on "if I cry." The Dm there is most effective when quiet

* Song Structure Note

No conventional verse/chorus — the song flows continuously through a suite of sections. Play through in order.

Bridge Note:

The [Fm] chord is the emotional gut-punch of the song.  The Ab gives it that minor/bittersweet color against the C major key.  Let it ring and don't rush it.

Finale note:

The ending [Dm7] → [G7] → [C] is a classic ii7–V7–I jazz cadence.  Play it slowly and let [C] ring out as a final resolution.
