** Tips

- LH plays root note in bass; RH plays full chord
- Fm adds dark color — the Ab is the key note (black key, one semitone below A)
- G7 resolves strongly to C; lean into that pull
- D7 is a secondary dominant — it pulls toward G/G7

* Advanced Techniques

** Inversions

Inversions keep the LH bass moving smoothly instead of jumping around.  Use these as voice-leading substitutes for root-position chords.  *LH plays the bass (lowest) note; RH plays all listed notes.*

Fingering notes:
- *1–2–5 on a first inversion triad*: the minor 3rd at the bottom (e.g., E–G, A–C) sits naturally under fingers 1 and 2; pinky stretches to the octave note
- *2–3–5 for Fm/Ab*: avoid putting the thumb on the black key Ab — index on Ab gives a more stable anchor
- *D7/F# (1st inv.)*: thumb on black key F# is acceptable here; the 3–5 stretch across C–D at the top is tight — keep the hand relaxed and let the pinky fall naturally

Voice leading tip:

In the verse progression C → Dm → G7 → C, try this bass line:

- C (root) → D (root) → G (root) → C (root)
- Or the smoother: C → F (Dm/F) → G → E (C/E)

** Extended & Substitution Chords

These add color without changing the harmonic function.  Drop them in wherever the base chord appears — the substitution is always optional and additive.

| Original | Extended | Full Notes              | Key Addition   | Character                          |
|----------+----------+-------------------------+----------------+------------------------------------|
| G7       | G9       | G – B – D – F – A       | A (major 9th)  | Warmer, more open dominant         |
| G7       | G13      | G – B – D – F – A – E   | E (major 13th) | Full jazz dominant; very lush      |
| C        | Cmaj7    | C – E – G – B           | B (major 7th)  | Dreamy, half-suspended             |
| C        | C6       | C – E – G – A           | A (major 6th)  | Vintage warmth; common in 50s/60s  |
| Dm       | Dm9      | D – F – A – C – E       | E (major 9th)  | Richer minor; more open            |
| Am       | Am7      | A – C – E – G           | G (minor 7th)  | Softer; blurs the Am → Dm boundary |
| F        | Fmaj7    | F – A – C – E           | E (major 7th)  | Shimmering, slightly suspended IV  |

*** G9

G9 takes G7 and adds the 9th (A) — one whole step above the octave.  The A softens the harshness of the tritone inside G7 (B against F) without removing it.

  #+BEGIN_EXAMPLE
  LH: G (bass)
  RH: B – F – A    (3rd, 7th, 9th — omit D)
  Fingering: 1 – 3 – 5
  #+END_EXAMPLE

The D is omitted — context supplies it.  B is the most critical note (it forms the tritone with F), and A on top opens the chord up.  The interval B–F is a tritone (6 semitones), so fingers 1–3 need a slight spread.  Use anywhere G7 appears for a warmer, less abrupt resolution into C.

*** G13

G13 adds the 13th (E) — count up every other note from G: G–B–D–F–A–C–E, and E is the 13th.  In practice you drop most of the middle to make it playable.

  #+BEGIN_EXAMPLE
  LH: G (bass)
  RH: B – F – A – E    (3rd, 7th, 9th, 13th — omit D and C entirely)
  #+END_EXAMPLE

B and F are the tritone core; A and E are the color tones stacked on top.  The span B → E is a major 7th above a tritone — if the stretch is uncomfortable, arpeggiate upward (B–F–A–E) rather than holding all four simultaneously.  Save this for the climactic G7 before the Bridge's return to C; it makes the resolution feel enormous.

*** Cmaj7

Cmaj7 adds B natural — the leading tone, one semitone below the root.  Because B hovers so close to C it creates gentle suspension without real dissonance.  It doesn't pull; it floats.

  #+BEGIN_EXAMPLE
  LH: C (bass)
  RH: E – G – B    (3rd, 5th, major 7th)
  Fingering: 1 – 2 – 4
  #+END_EXAMPLE

This is the recommended opener for Verse 1 ("A candy colored clown").  Plain C is stable and awake; Cmaj7 sounds half-dreaming.  Let the B ring — it is the most distinctive note.

For a fuller sound across both hands: LH plays C–E, RH plays G–B.  The B on top gives the chord its airy, unresolved quality.

*** C6

C6 adds A natural — the major 6th above C.  A already lives inside Am and Dm, so C6 has a nostalgic, blended quality, as if the chord hasn't quite committed to plain C major.

  #+BEGIN_EXAMPLE
  LH: C (bass)
  RH: E – G – A    (3rd, 5th, 6th)
  Fingering: 1 – 3 – 4
  #+END_EXAMPLE

Note: C6 (C–E–G–A) contains the exact same four notes as Am7 (A–C–E–G).  The only difference is the bass note.  C6 over a C bass feels like a warm, settled tonic.  Am7 over an A bass feels like a soft minor.  Use C6 at phrase endings where you want warmth without the suspension of Cmaj7.

*** Dm9

Dm9 adds E above the Dm7 chord — the major 9th.  E is a bright, open note that lifts the otherwise closed minor sound of Dm7.

  #+BEGIN_EXAMPLE
  LH: D (bass)
  RH: F – A – C – E    (minor 3rd, 5th, minor 7th, major 9th)
  Fingering: 1 – 2 – 3 – 5
  #+END_EXAMPLE

Most effective on the Dm7 in the Finale cadence (Dm7 → G7 → C).  Try the extended version: Dm9 → G13 → Cmaj7.  That is a full ii9–V13–Imaj7 jazz resolution — the most sophisticated ending available in this song.

*** Am7

Am7 adds G natural — the minor 7th above A.  G is the root of the dominant chord and appears throughout the key, so Am7 sounds like it belongs everywhere.

  #+BEGIN_EXAMPLE
  LH: A (bass)
  RH: C – E – G    (minor 3rd, 5th, minor 7th)
  Fingering: 1 – 3 – 5
  #+END_EXAMPLE

Note: Am7 (A–C–E–G) is the same four notes as C6 (C–E–G–A) — see C6 above.  Over an A bass it sounds fluid and minor; over a C bass it sounds like a warm tonic.  Am7 softens the vi chord and blurs the boundary of the Am → Dm movement, which suits the dreamlike quality of the song.

*** Fmaj7

Fmaj7 adds E natural — one semitone below the root F.  Like Cmaj7, this creates shimmer rather than tension.  E is also the 3rd of C major, so Fmaj7 shares notes with C and has a luminous, blended quality.

  #+BEGIN_EXAMPLE
  LH: F (bass)
  RH: A – C – E    (major 3rd, 5th, major 7th)
  Fingering: 1 – 2 – 5
  #+END_EXAMPLE

Most effective when F appears just before G7 ("Go to sleep, everything is all right").  The E on top of Fmaj7 sits one semitone below F, creating a close shimmer; the chord then releases into G7 and resolves to C.  The A–C–E in RH is also an Am triad, which reinforces the key's vi feeling even over an F bass.

** Rhythmic/Textural Ideas

- *Arpeggiate the chords* (broken chord, LH): root → 5th → octave while RH holds the triad
- *Ballad pattern* (LH): root on beat 1, chord on beats 2 and 3 (waltz-feel variation)
- *Sustained pedal tone*: hold G in the bass through the Bridge (Fm sections) for a suspended, unresolved tension that releases when C returns
- *Bridge dynamics*: drop to pp on "But just before the dawn," swell into the Fm, then pull back again on "if I cry." The Dm there is most effective when quiet

* Song Structure Note

No conventional verse/chorus — the song flows continuously through a suite of sections. Play through in order.

Bridge Note:

The [Fm] chord is the emotional gut-punch of the song.  The Ab gives it that minor/bittersweet color against the C major key.  Let it ring and don't rush it.

Finale note:

The ending [Dm7] → [G7] → [C] is a classic ii7–V7–I jazz cadence.  Play it slowly and let [C] ring out as a final resolution.
