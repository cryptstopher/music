* Key & Theory

Glycerine lives entirely inside one key: F major.  The whole song is built from a single four-chord loop, F – C – Dm – Bb, which is the classic I – V – vi – IV pop progression, the same family as "Let It Be" and "Don't Stop Believin'."  What makes the song interesting isn't harmonic complexity, it's arrangement: the verses stay hushed and sparse, then the exact same four chords open up into the full-band chorus.

** Diatonic Triads in F Major

| Degree | Chord | Notes      | Used in song? |
|--------+-------+------------+---------------|
| I      | F     | F – A – C  | Yes           |
| ii     | Gm    | G – Bb – D | No            |
| iii    | Am    | A – C – E  | No            |
| IV     | Bb    | Bb – D – F | Yes           |
| V      | C     | C – E – G  | Yes           |
| vi     | Dm    | D – F – A  | Yes           |
| vii°   | Edim  | E – G – Bb | No            |

The verse/chorus loop cycles I → V → vi → IV (F → C → Dm → Bb).  The Bridge strips this back to just vi → IV (Dm → Bb), which is part of why it feels more unsettled, there's no I chord to land on.

** Tips

- All four chords are simple root-position triads, no exotic voicings needed.
- LH can hold single roots or root+fifth; RH carries block chords or arpeggiates for the sparser verses.
- Keep the *same hand shape* moving F → C → Dm → Bb, this loop repeats for almost the entire song, so economy of motion matters more than fancy voicings.

| Chord | Position      | Notes (low to high) | When to Use                              |

| F/A   | 1st inversion | A – C – F            | Smooths the move into or out of Dm       |
| F/C   | 2nd inversion | C – F – A            | Pedal C in the bass under the tonic      |

| C/E   | 1st inversion | E – G – C            | Walks up smoothly from F                 |
| C/G   | 2nd inversion | G – C – E            | Pedal G; sets up the move to Dm          |

| Dm    | Root          | D – F – A            | Default vi chord                         |
| Dm/F  | 1st inversion | F – A – D            | Shares F with the tonic; smooth link     |

| Bb    | Root          | Bb – D – F           | Default IV chord                         |

* Performance Notes

- *Tempo*: Mid-tempo, roughly 76–80 BPM, steady and unhurried.
- *Dynamics*: This is the whole arrangement.  Verses are hushed, almost a whisper, hands close to the keys.  Choruses open up to full block chords with more pedal.
- *Touch*: Play the verse voicings soft and legato; let the chorus voicings ring out.
- *Pedal*: Sparing in the verses (or none at all); a full sustain wash under the chorus.
- *The tag*: The "Glycerine, Glycerine" hook rides the same F–C–Bb shape every time, it's the song's emotional release valve, let it breathe.
- *Bridge*: The Dm–Bb-only bridge should feel like it's floating, there's no I chord to resolve to, it's the most unsettled moment in the song.
- *Practice loop*:
  1. Loop just F → C → Dm → Bb slowly until the hand shape is automatic.
  2. Add the verse dynamic (quiet, sparse).
  3. Play the same four chords with the chorus dynamic (full, pedaled).
  4. Layer in the Bridge's Dm–Bb-only section.
