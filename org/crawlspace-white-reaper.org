* Introduction

"Crawlspace" is a bright, hook-stuffed cut from White Reaper's 2023 album
/Asking for a Ride/, the record where the Louisville band dialed the polish
back down and leaned into fast, scrappy, guitar-forward power-pop.  The track
is all sugar on the
surface, a four-chord pop loop, big handclap energy, a chorus you can shout
after one listen.  Then you hear the words: "There's no more room in my crawl
space / I'm gonna have to incinerate you, love."  The whole song runs on that
contrast, a serial-killer confession sung like a love letter, set to the
sunniest progression the band could find.  Lean into the brightness when you
play it; the menace takes care of itself.

Harmonically there is almost nothing here, which is the point.  Four chords,
all root-position triads, all diatonic, looped relentlessly.  The work for the
pianist is rhythmic, not harmonic: make four chords feel like a freight train.

* Capo & Transposition (read this first)

The original guitar chart is labeled *"Capo on 6th fret and transpose to G for
open chords."*  Here is exactly what that means, and why it does **not** change
what you play on piano.

- A guitarist clamps a capo on the 6th fret and plays easy open-position
  shapes as if the song were in *G major*: =G=, =Em=, =C=, =D=.
- The capo raises every string 6 semitones, so those shapes do not *sound*
  like G, Em, C, D.  They sound a tritone higher: =C#=, =A#m=, =F#=, =G#=.
- Those sounding pitches, *not* the shapes, are what the chart prints above
  the lyrics.  The chart is already in concert pitch.
- A piano has no capo and no "shapes."  The pianist just plays the real,
  sounding chords.  To avoid a thicket of sharps, respell them in flats.

| Guitar shape (capo 6) | Sounds as | Piano spelling |
|-----------------------+-----------+----------------|
| G                     | C#        | *Db*           |
| Em                    | A#m       | *Bbm*          |
| C                     | F#        | *Gb*           |
| D                     | G#        | *Ab*           |

⚠️ *Do not play G–Em–C–D on the piano.*  That is the guitarist's capo
illusion.  The actual music is in *Db major*, and your four chords are
*Db, Bbm, Gb, Ab*.  A capo-6 guitarist and a no-capo pianist playing these
side by side will be in perfect unison.

* Key & Theory

The song lives in *Db major* (= C# major; flats are far kinder to read).
Every chord is diatonic.

| Chord | Role | Comment                                  |
|-------+------+------------------------------------------|
| Db    | I    | tonic, the home of the verse and chorus  |
| Bbm   | vi   | the relative minor, the "sad" pivot      |
| Gb    | IV   | the lift                                 |
| Ab    | V    | the dominant, only appears on "incinerate" |

The verse pairs *Db ↔ Bbm* (I ↔ vi) and then *Gb ↔ Bbm* (IV ↔ vi), ending
each half on a *Gb → Ab* (IV → V) push under "send you my love."  The chorus
is the same *Db ↔ Bbm* with a single *Gb Ab Db Gb* turnaround on "incinerate
you, love," then a hypnotic *Db ↔ Gb* (I ↔ IV) rock on the word "Love."  The
final chorus extends that I ↔ IV oscillation under the closing "There's no
more room in my crawl space" tag.

Notice the song never resolves *V → I* in a cadential way.  The dominant Ab is
a quick passing stab, not an arrival.  The home of the song is the I ↔ vi and
I ↔ IV rocking; it feels perpetually mid-motion, which suits a song about
never quite finishing the job.

| vii°   | Cdim  | C, Eb, Gb  | no            |

Four of the seven triads, the I, IV, V, and vi, do all the work.  This is the
"four chords that built pop music" set, just sitting on Db instead of C or G.

** Tips

- LH plays the root in the bass; RH holds the triad.  No extensions, no
  sevenths, this is a triad song.  Resist the urge to jazz it up
- *Db → Bbm* is two common tones.  Db (Db F Ab) → Bbm (Bb Db F): keep *Db*
  and *F* exactly where they are; only Ab nudges up to Bb.  Your right hand
  barely moves.  Let the bass (Db → Bb) carry the change
- *Gb → Bbm* is also two common tones.  Gb (Gb Bb Db) → Bbm (Bb Db F): keep
  *Bb* and *Db*; only Gb steps down to F.  Again, the right hand is nearly
  still
- *Gb → Ab* (IV → V, the "incinerate" push) has no common tones, every voice
  jumps.  This is intentional; it is the one place the harmony lurches.  Hit
  it hard and let it feel like a shove
- *Db → Gb* (the "Love" rock) shares *Db*.  Keep Db on top, swing the rest:
  Db F Ab → Db Gb Bb is a tidy way to oscillate without leaping all over the
  keyboard

* Inversions for Voice Leading

The whole song rocks between two or three chords in tight succession.
Inversions keep the right hand from sprinting up and down the keyboard and
let the common tones ring.

| Chord | Position | Notes (low→high) | When to Use                                |
|-------+----------+------------------+--------------------------------------------|
| Db    | Root     | Db, F, Ab        | Default tonic                              |
| Db/F  | 1st inv. | F, Ab, Db        | F in bass walks toward Gb                  |
| Db/Ab | 2nd inv. | Ab, Db, F        | Ab pedal, ties Db into Ab (V)              |
|-------+----------+------------------+--------------------------------------------|
| Bbm   | Root     | Bb, Db, F        | Default vi                                 |
| Bbm/Db| 1st inv. | Db, F, Bb        | Db & F held from Db chord; only top moves  |
| Bbm/F | 2nd inv. | F, Bb, Db        | F pedal from Gb chord                       |
|-------+----------+------------------+--------------------------------------------|
| Gb    | Root     | Gb, Bb, Db       | Default IV                                 |
| Gb/Bb | 1st inv. | Bb, Db, Gb       | Bb & Db held from Bbm                       |
| Gb/Db | 2nd inv. | Db, Gb, Bb       | Db pedal, ties Db into Gb on "Love"        |
|-------+----------+------------------+--------------------------------------------|
| Ab    | Root     | Ab, C, Eb        | Default V; play it punchy in root position |

Voice-leading recipe for the verse:

Play *Db* in root position, then *Bbm in first inversion* (=Db, F, Bb=).  The
right hand keeps Db and F dead still and only lifts Ab → Bb.  The bass moves
Db → Bb.  The chord changes but the texture does not flinch, perfect for a
verse that needs to drive without distracting from the vocal.

For the "Love" tag, sit *Db* in root position and play *Gb in second
inversion* (=Db, Gb, Bb=).  Db stays on top of both chords as a pedal; the
inner voices rock F/Ab → Gb/Bb.  Minimal motion, maximum hypnosis.

* Performance Notes

- *Tempo*: fast and driving, roughly *150 BPM*.  This is a runaway pop song,
  not a ballad.  The energy is the instrument
- *Touch*: percussive and rhythmic.  Think eighth-note or steady quarter-note
  chord stabs, not held pads.  On piano this song is closer to a drummer's
  job than a balladeer's.  Punch the chords, release them, punch again
- *Pedal*: use it sparingly or not at all.  A garage-pop track wants clarity
  and bite, not a wash.  If you pedal, change it cleanly on every chord and
  keep it shallow
- *Dynamics*: there is almost no quiet section.  Verse and chorus sit at
  nearly the same loud, bright level.  The lift into the chorus is energy and
  density, not volume, fill in more of the beat, widen the voicing, maybe
  add an octave in the left hand
- *The verse* (=Db Bbm / Db Bbm / Gb Bbm / Gb Bbm / Gb Ab=): keep it
  relentless and even.  The =Gb → Ab= at the end of each half ("send you my
  love") is the only harmonic surprise, give it a little extra weight so it
  pops out of the loop
- *The chorus* (=Db Bbm / Db Bbm / Db Bbm / Gb Ab Db Gb=): the
  =Gb Ab Db Gb= under "incinerate you, love" moves fast, four chords in two
  bars.  Lock those changes tight; this is the hook's payoff
- *The "Love" tag* (=Db Gb / Db Gb / Db Gb=): a hypnotic I ↔ IV rock.  Keep
  it dead steady, almost mechanical.  This is a chant, not a phrase
- *The outro* extends the chorus, adding four bars of =Db Gb= under "There's
  no more room in my crawl space."  Same I ↔ IV rock as the "Love" tag.
  Ride it out at full energy and let the band (or your left hand) cut off
  abruptly rather than fading, this song stops, it does not dissolve
