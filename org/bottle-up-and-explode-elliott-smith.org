* Introduction

"Bottle Up and Explode!" is track 11 on Elliott Smith's 1998 album /XO/, his
first release for a major label (DreamWorks) after three albums of
lo-fi, mostly solo-acoustic recording. Where a lot of /XO/ sits close to a
whisper, this one is a full-band, driving rock arrangement, drums, bass, and
a steady eighth-note pulse under the vocal, closer in energy to "Baby
Britain" than to the hushed a cappella of "I Didn't Understand" at the
album's close.

The title is the thesis: the harmony spends most of its time circling *C#
minor* (the relative minor of the song's home key, E major) rather than
resolving cleanly to E, and it keeps reaching for chromatic, "wrong note"
major chords, G# major and F# major, that don't belong to the home key at
all. That tension between a minor-feeling center and bright, borrowed major
chords pushing against it is a very Elliott Smith harmonic fingerprint, and
it mirrors the lyric's theme of repression and eventual eruption.

* Key & Theory

The song's home key is *E major*, but it rarely lands there for long. The
verse's gravitational center is *C#m* (vi), and the harmony repeatedly
detours through *G# major* and *F# major*, chords borrowed from *outside*
the E major scale, functioning as *secondary dominants* pointing at C#m and
B respectively.

| Chord | Role                    | Comment                                             |
|-------+-------------------------+------------------------------------------------------|
| E     | I                       | home key, mostly passed through, never lingered on  |
| A     | IV                      | the harmonic "resting point" between phrases        |
| B     | V                       | true dominant, arrives at the hook ("seeing stars") |
| C#m   | vi                      | the real tonal center of the verse                  |
| G#m   | iii                     | diatonic, but only used briefly (the hook's cadence) |
| G#    | V/vi (borrowed)         | secondary dominant, pulls hard toward C#m           |
| F#    | V/V (borrowed)          | secondary dominant, pulls toward B                  |
| Esus4 | Isus4                   | suspended color chord, decorates E                  |
| B/C#  | V (bass walk-up)        | B major held while the bass steps up to C#          |

The *verse* opens *C#m → A → E* (vi → IV → I), a descending motion away from
the minor center that never quite resolves the way a I-based song would.
It then reaches for *G# → A* (V/vi → IV), a chromatic pull toward C#m that
deceptively resolves to IV instead, and *F# → A → Esus4* (V/V → IV → Isus4),
the same trick aimed at B instead. The pattern repeats, then the hook lands
on *B → B/C# → C#m → G#m* (V → V(bass walk) → vi → iii), the closest thing
the song has to a cadence, resolving downward into the minor rather than up
into the major.

** Diatonic Triads in E Major

| Degree | Chord  | Notes      | Used in song?                                |
|--------+--------+------------+-----------------------------------------------|
| I      | E      | E, G#, B   | yes                                           |
| ii     | F#m    | F#, A, C#  | *no* — replaced by borrowed F# major          |
| iii    | G#m    | G#, B, D#  | yes, briefly (the hook's cadence)             |
| IV     | A      | A, C#, E   | yes                                           |
| V      | B      | B, D#, F#  | yes                                           |
| vi     | C#m    | C#, E, G#  | yes, the real tonal center                    |
| vii°   | D#dim  | D#, F#, A  | no                                             |

Note that the song never plays the diatonic *ii* (F#m); it uses *F# major*
instead, a chromatic secondary dominant of B. Likewise *G# major* (not
diatonic at all in E major) is used far more than the diatonic *iii* (G#m),
which only shows up once, briefly, as part of the hook's cadence. This
substitution of borrowed major chords for their diatonic minor relatives is
what gives the song its restless, can't-settle-down harmonic character.

** Tips

- *C#m → A*: two common tones. C#m (C# E G#) → A (A C# E): keep *C#* and *E*
  exactly where they are, only *G#* steps up a half step to *A*
- *A → E*: only one common tone (*E*), but it's worth holding steady in the
  same voice while the rest of the hand resets underneath it
- *G# → A*: this is the "wrong note pulling home" moment. G# (G# C D#) → A
  (A C# E): the *G#* itself resolves up a half step into the *A* that
  follows, textbook secondary-dominant motion. Lean into it slightly
- *F# → A*: F# (F# A# C#) → A (A C# E): keep *C#* common, *F#* falls to *E*
  eventually across the phrase, and *A#* resolves up to *B* if you voice it
  that way in a later chord. Treat F# as a brief, bright detour, not a
  destination
- *B → B/C#*: the right hand doesn't move at all, only the bass steps up a
  whole step from B to C#, setting up the vi chord that follows
- *C#m → G#m*: both are minor triads a fourth apart; C#m (C# E G#) and G#m
  (G# B D#) share the single common tone *G#* — keep it anchored while the
  other two voices shift

** Esus4

#+BEGIN_EXAMPLE
LH: E   (root, low)
RH: E - A - B   (root, 4th, 5th — the suspended 4th replaces the 3rd)

The song doesn't resolve this down to a full E triad; it's left hanging as
a color chord before the next line picks back up. Don't rush it.
#+END_EXAMPLE

** B/C#

#+BEGIN_EXAMPLE
LH: C#   (bass note — NOT the chord root)
RH: B - D# - F#   (full B major triad, unchanged from the B chord before it)

Only the bass moves, stepping up from B to C#. The right hand holds the
same B major shape both chords; the moving bass is what sets up C#m.
#+END_EXAMPLE

* Inversions for Voice Leading

| Chord | Position | Notes (low→high) | When to Use                                 |
|-------+----------+-------------------+----------------------------------------------|
| C#m   | Root     | C#, E, G#         | Default vi, verse opening                    |
| C#m/E | 1st inv. | E, G#, C#         | E held from the previous E chord             |
| C#m/G#| 2nd inv. | G#, C#, E         | G# pedal, ties into a following G#/G#m       |
|-------+----------+-------------------+------------------------------------------------|
| A     | Root     | A, C#, E          | Default IV, the phrase's resting point       |
| A/C#  | 1st inv. | C#, E, A          | C# held from C#m; only one voice moves       |
| A/E   | 2nd inv. | E, A, C#          | E pedal between two E-containing chords      |
|-------+----------+-------------------+------------------------------------------------|
| E     | Root     | E, G#, B          | Default I, mostly passed through             |
| E/G#  | 1st inv. | G#, B, E          | G# held from A or G#/G#m                     |
| E/B   | 2nd inv. | B, E, G#          | B pedal, sets up the following B chord       |
|-------+----------+-------------------+------------------------------------------------|
| B     | Root     | B, D#, F#         | Default V, arrives at the hook               |
| B/D#  | 1st inv. | D#, F#, B         | smoother top voice into C#m (D# ~ E neighbor) |
| B/F#  | 2nd inv. | F#, B, D#         | F# pedal, echoes the F# chord before it      |
|-------+----------+-------------------+------------------------------------------------|
| G#    | Root     | G#, C, D#         | Default borrowed V/vi                        |
| G#/C  | 1st inv. | C, D#, G#         | smoother resolution, C steps to C# (C#m root) |
|-------+----------+-------------------+------------------------------------------------|
| F#    | Root     | F#, A#, C#        | Default borrowed V/V                         |
| F#/C# | 1st inv. | C#, F#, A#        | C# held from the surrounding C#m/A chords    |

Voice-leading recipe for the verse: play *C#m* in root position, then *A* in
first inversion (=C#, E, A=) so only one voice moves (G# up to nothing new,
C# and E just hold), then *E* in second inversion (=B, E, G#=) so the B
carries straight into the next phrase. When *G#* or *F#* show up, treat them
as brief leaning-tones, resolve the top voice down or up by a half step into
the chord that follows rather than jumping to a fresh root-position shape.

For the hook (*B → B/C# → C#m → G#m*), keep the right hand's B major shape
completely still across the first two chords and let the left-hand bass do
all the work walking up from B to C#. Then let the top voice fall from F# to
G at the C#m, and finally to a *G#m* voicing that shares its G# with the
chord before it.

* Performance Notes

- *Tempo*: a driving, moderate rock tempo, roughly *130-140 BPM*, with a
  steady eighth-note feel, unlike the rubato ballads elsewhere on /XO/
- *Touch*: firmer and more rhythmic than a ballad voicing. This is a full
  band arrangement; don't be shy about a percussive left hand
- *Pedal*: light, clear it on every chord change, especially through the
  G# and F# secondary dominants so they don't blur into the chord before
  or after them
- *Dynamics*: the verses sit at a controlled, simmering mezzo-forte; the
  title's whole idea is something held down, so don't let it boil over
  until the hook
- *The verse* (=C#m A E / G# A / F# A Esus4=): keep the left hand steady
  and metronomic under the chromatic G#/F# detours, they should feel like
  reaching, not arriving
- *The hook* (=B B/C# C#m G#m=): this is the closest thing to a release the
  song offers, lean into the bass walk-up under "seeing stars" / "if you're
  gonna hide," it's the emotional high point of each verse
- *The solo* (=B B/C# C#m G#m / E A / E F# G#=, repeating on "becoming
  you"): let the repeated E-F#-G# figure climb and build with each pass,
  it's the closest the song gets to its own explosion
