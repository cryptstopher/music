* Introduction

Written by Glenn Danzig for the /Less Than Zero/ soundtrack (1987) and performed by Roy Orbison, one of his final recordings.  An unlikely pairing on paper (the founder of the Misfits and the operatic balladeer of "Crying"), but Danzig's gothic imagery lands perfectly inside Orbison's vibrato.  The song is slow, sparse, and resigned.  Play it accordingly.

* Key & Theory

The song lives in *E major*.  The chord palette is small, six chords total, and almost entirely diatonic.  The only chord that steps outside basic triads is *Bsus4*, which appears once, in the Bridge, and serves as the song's only true tension moment before the long resignation of the outro.

Despite the major key, the mood is funereal.  This comes from two things: the heavy use of *C#m* (the relative minor / vi chord) at the start of nearly every section, and the unhurried tempo.  E major is the destination, but the song keeps drifting toward C#m as if it doesn't want to arrive.

The song uses five of the seven diatonic chords, plus one suspension (Bsus4).  No borrowed chords, no secondary dominants, the harmonic sadness comes from voicing and pacing, not exotic chord choices.

** Tips

- LH plays the root in the bass; RH holds the triad
- *E → C#m* is the song's signature move, the bass drops a major third (E → C#) and only one note changes (B → C#).  Voice this transition with minimal motion in the right hand
- *C#m → A* is just as smooth, A and C# are common to both chords, only G# moves down to A in one voice
- The B chord almost always resolves down to E.  When it appears as Bsus4 in the Bridge, the suspended E (the 4th) resolves into the E chord that follows, that *is* the resolution
- *N.C.* in the chart means "no chord", vocal phrases over silence or sparse texture.  On piano, hold the previous chord under sustain or rest entirely

** The Bsus4 Chord

Bsus4 replaces the 3rd of B major (D#) with the 4th (E).  The suspension creates tension because E wants to fall back to D# to complete the B chord, but here it doesn't.  Instead, the chord moves directly to E major, which means the suspended E note becomes the *root* of the next chord.  That is the entire emotional payoff of the Bridge.

  #+BEGIN_EXAMPLE
  LH: B (bass)
  RH: B – E – F#    (root, 4th, 5th, D# replaced by E)
  Fingering: 1 – 3 – 5
  #+END_EXAMPLE

The motion *Bsus4 → E* is sometimes called a "plagal-like" resolution because the 4th in the chord becomes the root of the resolution.  It feels like exhaling.

* Inversions for Voice Leading

Because the song moves between E and C#m so often, smoothing the bass line with inversions makes a real difference.  *LH plays the bass (lowest) note; RH plays the rest.*

| Chord   | Position  | Notes (low → high) | When to Use                                       |
|---------+-----------+--------------------+---------------------------------------------------|
| E       | Root      | E – G# – B         | Default tonic; section openers                    |
| E/G#    | 1st inv.  | G# – B – E         | G# in bass walks down to F#m (G# → F#)            |
| E/B     | 2nd inv.  | B – E – G#         | B in bass holds from preceding B chord            |
|---------+-----------+--------------------+---------------------------------------------------|
| C#m     | Root      | C# – E – G#        | Default vi                                        |
| C#m/E   | 1st inv.  | E – G# – C#        | E in bass keeps the tonic note alive under C#m    |
| C#m/G#  | 2nd inv.  | G# – C# – E        | G# pedal, extremely smooth from E (root)         |
|---------+-----------+--------------------+---------------------------------------------------|
| A       | Root      | A – C# – E         | Default IV                                        |
| A/C#    | 1st inv.  | C# – E – A         | C# in bass continues from C#m                     |
| A/E     | 2nd inv.  | E – A – C#         | E pedal; ties A back to E tonic                   |
|---------+-----------+--------------------+---------------------------------------------------|
| B       | Root      | B – D# – F#        | Default V                                         |
| B/D#    | 1st inv.  | D# – F# – B        | D# rising to E creates a chromatic step           |
| B/F#    | 2nd inv.  | F# – B – D#        | F# pedal                                          |
|---------+-----------+--------------------+---------------------------------------------------|
| F#m     | Root      | F# – A – C#        | Default ii                                        |
| F#m/A   | 1st inv.  | A – C# – F#        | A in bass walks up to B                           |
| F#m/C#  | 2nd inv.  | C# – F# – A        | C# pedal, voice-leads from A or C#m              |

Voice-leading tip:

In the verse cycle *E → C#m → A → B → E*, try this bass line:

- E → C# → A → B → E (each note from the chord root)
- Or smoother: E → C# → C# (A/C#) → B → E, keeps the bass on C# for two beats

* Performance Notes

- *Tempo*: slow.  Around 70 BPM.  This is a death song; do not push.
- *Touch*: soft.  Stay in the *p* to *mp* range almost throughout.  The "yeah, yeah, yeah" outro can swell slightly but never breaks into forte.
- *Pedal*: change on every chord.  Let each chord ring fully, silence between chords is part of the song
- *N.C. sections*: in the Bridge, vocal phrases land over silence or a single held note.  Resist the urge to fill, let the voice (or imagined voice) carry the line alone
- *The Bsus4*: lean on it.  This is the only moment of unresolved tension in the song.  Hold it a beat longer than the chart suggests, then let it fall into E
- *The outro*: the final phrase "One last thing to say, life fades a-way" sits over a sustained E chord.  The chart shows E repeated many times, this is one held E, sometimes re-struck softly to keep the sound alive.  Hold the sustain pedal down through the entire phrase and let the final chord decay all the way to silence

** Bridge 1

  #+BEGIN_EXAMPLE
  N.C.          [C#m]    N.C.            [A]
  I'm tired of tomorrow, lost for today
  N.C.       [Bsus4]            [E]
  I long to be at peace forever
  #+END_EXAMPLE

Bridge note:

The *Bsus4* is the song's only sharp turn.  E (the suspended 4th in Bsus4) is held over from the preceding A chord, the bass moves A → B underneath but the E note in the right hand stays put.  When the chord resolves to E major, that same E note becomes the root.  One note never moves; everything around it shifts.  This is the closest the song comes to an emotional climax.

** Chorus / Outro

  #+BEGIN_EXAMPLE
       [F#m]
  Forever more
       [A]                          [B]
  Oh yeah, yeah, yeah, yeah, yeah
  [A]                          [B]
  Yeah, yeah, yeah, yeah, yeah
  [A]                          [B]
  Yeah, yeah, yeah, yeah, yeah
       [E]                       [F#m]
  Life fades away, Life fades a-way
       [A]   [B]      [C#m]   N.C.
  Life fades away,    away
  [A]                  [B]      [E] [E] [E] [E] [E] [E] [E] [E] [E] [E] [E] [E] [E]
  One last thing to say, life fades     a     -     way
  #+END_EXAMPLE

Outro note:

The repeated *E* chords on the final line are not separate strikes, they are one sustained E with the singer's phrase drawn out over the top.  On piano, hold the chord with the sustain pedal and re-strike softly only if the sound starts to die.  The final word ("way") should land as the chord is decaying, let it ring into silence.  Do not lift the pedal until the sound is fully gone.
