* Introduction

Oh, wow.
What a hauntingly beautiful song from Elliott Smith's final studio album, /From a Basement on the Hill/ (2004).
You can listen to the [[https://youtu.be/Uw01bVaNd7s?si=MOEbMW_Gph2pGqNw][song here]].

The cheat sheet here provides some simplified chords for playing this song on the piano.
It stays in a fairly ~C major~ scale, but with a sad ~A minor~ feel.

* Chords

| Chord   | Degree | Notes    | Voicing (Low → High) |
|---------+--------+----------+----------------------|
| Fmaj7/C | IVmaj7 | F A C E  | C (bass) / F A C E   |
| Am      | vi     | A C E    | A (bass) / A C E     |
| G       | V      | G B D    | G (bass) / G B D     |
| F       | IV     | F A C    | F (bass) / F A C     |
| C/G     | I      | C E G    | G (bass) / C E G     |
| D7      | V7/V * | D F# A C | D (bass) / F# A C    |

D7 is a secondary dominant: borrows F# from outside C major to pull toward the G chord.

* Key & theory

- Key: C major / A minor
- Feel: Bittersweet, suspended, introspective
- The Fmaj7/C is the emotional anchor, that E on top gives it an airy, unresolved quality
- D7 is the tension chord; the F# pulls hard toward resolution
- C/G acts as a passing chord — the G bass creates forward motion without a full cadence

* Scales & Improvisation

** Primary Scales

| Scale              | Notes           | Use When                      |
|--------------------+-----------------+-------------------------------|
| A natural minor    | A B C D E F G   | Whole song — default          |
| A minor pentatonic | A C D E G       | Safest starting point         |
| C major            | C D E F G A B   | Same notes, brighter emphasis |

** Notes to Emphasize Over Each Chord

- Fmaj7/C :: F, A, C, E — the E on top is the most distinctive note; lean on it
- Am :: A, C, E — home base; anything in the minor scale sounds right
- G :: G, B, D — B natural is outside the minor pentatonic but essential here
- F :: F, A, C — warm and grounded; A and C are safe landing notes
- C/G :: C, E, G — E sounds bright; land on G for a gentle passing feel
- D7 :: D, A — safe anchors; F# (the raised 7th) adds tension — use sparingly

** The D7 Exception

D7 contains F#, which sits outside A natural minor. Two options:

1. Avoid F# — stay on D, A, C. It still works.
2. Use F# as a brief passing note rising to G — it pulls hard upward and resolves beautifully.

** Improvisation Tips for Beginners

- Start with A minor pentatonic (A C D E G) — only 5 notes, all safe, all sound good
- Play slowly and leave gaps — Elliott Smith's melodic style is sparse, not busy
- Land on chord tones at the end of phrases (the notes of whichever chord is playing)
- The note E is especially resonant over Fmaj7/C — return to it often
- Avoid rushing to fill silence; the rests are part of the melody

* Song Structure

- Verse :: Fmaj7/C → Am → G progression
- The chord movement stays close — voice lead smoothly, minimal hand movement
- Note on the D7 :: sheet says you can substitute something on this chord; let it breathe

* Style Notes

** Elliott Smith Piano Approach
- Sparse > dense. Leave space.
- Let bass notes sustain while inner voices move
- Avoid heavy attack — aim for a soft, almost hesitant touch
- The melody sits on top; chord tones underneath, not competing

** Voicing Tips
- Drop the 5th from most chords — it still sounds full on piano
- Fmaj7/C: left hand C alone, right hand F-A-E is especially open and lovely
- Keep right hand voicings in the middle register (not too high)

** Dynamics
- Stay mostly in the p to mp range
- Swell slightly on the G chord resolutions
- D7 can have a little more weight to emphasize the tension
