Introduction

"You Were Meant for Me" is the breakout single from Jewel's 1995 debut album Pieces of You, co-written with her one-time tour partner Steve Poltz. Re-released in 1996 as a double A-side with "Foolish Games," it became one of the longest-charting singles in Billboard Hot 100 history, a slow-burn hit for an album that had been out for over a year.

No capo is indicated on the source chart, so the chords below are the real, sounding pitches, no transposition needed. The one thing worth knowing going in: this is not a plain four-chord folk song. There is a secondary-dominant slash chord, C#/F, that drives a chromatic bass walk-down in the chorus and bridge, and it is the harmonic detail that makes the song feel like it is gently pulling itself forward.

Key & Theory

The song lives in B major. Most of the harmony is diatonic, but the chart also borrows one chord, C#, from outside the key as a secondary dominant, plus a passing A#m in the bridge.

Chord Role Comment
Badd9 I tonic, colored with an added 9th (C#)
F#6/A# V (1st inv) dominant, A# (the 3rd) in the bass, 6th = D#
D#m iii diatonic, closes off the intro/verse loop
C# V/V borrowed, secondary dominant of F#
C#/F V/V (inv.) same chord, E# ("F") in the bass, passing tone
A#m vi of C# borrowed, brief color chord in the bridge

The verse/intro loop is Badd9 → F#6/A# → B → D#m, essentially I → V → I → iii, kept in constant motion by the inverted V chord in the bass. The chorus and bridge both lean on B → C# → F# → C#/F → D#m: the C# is not the diatonic ii (which would be C#m), it is a secondary dominant, the V of F#, borrowed to intensify the pull toward the V chord. Landing on C#/F right after F# puts E# (spelled "F" on the chart) in the bass, and the bass line quietly walks F# → E# → D#, three descending half/whole steps that glide straight into D#m. This one move is the harmonic signature of the song, the same role the "Bm → Bm/C# → D" walk-up plays in The Cars' "You Might Think," just descending instead of ascending.

Tips

  • LH takes the bass note (root, or the slash-chord bass tone for the inversions); RH carries the triad. Badd9 is the only chord that needs a 4th right-hand finger, for the added 9th (C#)
  • F#6/A# : keep the RH shape F# A# C# D# (or just A# C# D# if you drop the low F#) and put A# in the LH. This is what keeps the intro/verse loop feeling like it is gently rocking rather than changing chords
  • C# and C#/F are the same right hand, C# E# G#. Only the bass note moves, F# in the LH for the F# chord that comes just before, then E# (written "F" on the chart), then D# for the D#m that follows. Practice that LH line alone: F# → E# → D#, slow half/whole steps
  • B → C# (I → V/V) has one common tone, F#. B (B D# F#) → C# (C# E# G#): F# moves down a half step to E#, D# moves up a half step to E# too? no, keep it simple: think of it as the whole hand sliding up a whole step, B→C#, D#→E#, F#→G#
  • A#m is a passing color chord. Don't linger, it resolves quickly down into D#m; think of it as leaning briefly into a darker color before settling back home

Inversions for Voice Leading

The signature move in this song is the F# → C#/F → D#m bass walk, so the inversions below are built to keep that line smooth and the right hand as still as possible.

Chord Position Notes (low→high) When to Use
B Root B, D#, F# Default tonic
B/D# 1st inv. D#, F#, B D# pedal, ties B into D#m
B/F# 2nd inv. F#, B, D# F# held from the F# chord
F# Root F#, A#, C# Default V
F#/A# 1st inv. A#, C#, F# matches the chart's "F#6/A#" bass
F#6 Root+6th F#, A#, C#, D# adds D# on top for extra color
C# Root C#, E#, G# Default V/V
C#/F 1st inv. E#(F), G#, C# the chart's passing walk chord
D#m Root D#, F#, A# Default iii, resolution point
D#m/F# 1st inv. F#, A#, D# F# held over from F# or C#/F
D#m/A# 2nd inv. A#, D#, F# A# pedal, ties in from A#m

Voice-leading recipe for the chorus turnaround:

Play F# in root position (F# A# C#), then slide the bass down a half step to E# for C#/F while the right hand barely moves, C# and A# stay in the neighborhood, G# is a half step from A#. Then walk the bass down another half/whole step to D# for D#m, and let the right hand settle into D# F# A#. The whole gesture should feel like one continuous downward slide, not three separate chord grabs.

Performance Notes

  • Tempo: mid-tempo, a gentle folk-pop pulse around 76 BPM. Nothing here should feel rushed, the verses are almost spoken-word in places
  • Touch: legato throughout. This is a strummed-acoustic-guitar song on the record, but on piano let the chords sustain into one another, especially through the F# → C#/F → D#m walk
  • Pedal: light-to-moderate. Change it on every chord in the verse to keep the words clear, and let it breathe a little more under the chorus once the vocal opens up
  • Dynamics: verses are conversational and a little wry (the eggs, the pancakes, the wet towels), keep them light and almost talky. The chorus should open up and warm, without turning into a belt, this song stays intimate even at its biggest moments
  • The intro/verse loop (Badd9 F#6/A# B D#m): keep the left hand's rocking bass steady, it is the engine of the whole song
  • The chorus (B C# F# C#/F D#m ... B C# D#m): lean into the F# → C#/F → D#m bass walk, it is the emotional hinge of the progression. Let "you were meant for me" land softly on the bare B chord, this is the one moment the harmony simplifies
  • The bridge: the A#m is the only chord in the whole song that isn't diatonic to B major or a straightforward secondary dominant, treat it as a brief shadow before "hearts are broken every day" settles back onto D#m
  • The outro: let the final Badd9 F#6/A# B D#m loop trail off, the recording fades rather than ending on a hard cadence, so don't force a big final chord
