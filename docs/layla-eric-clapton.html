Key & Theory

The song lives in two tonal worlds:

  • Intro / Chorus / Instrumental: D minor (D – E – F – G – A – Bb – C)
  • Verses: C# minor (C# – D# – E – F# – G# – A – B)

The intro riff is the song's anchor. The verse shifts down a semitone to C# minor, giving it a raw, desperate quality. The chorus pulls back into D minor to reunite the two worlds.

The raised 7th (C#) creates a leading tone that pulls strongly back to D. This is why the A major chord (with C# inside it) sounds so natural in D minor, it borrows from the harmonic minor.

Use the pentatonic as your safe zone over the intro/chorus/instrumental. Add Bb and E when you want to color outside the lines.

Notes to Emphasize Over Each Verse Chord

C#m7
C#, E, G#, B, linger on B (the 7th) for a brooding, unresolved feel
G#7
G#, C (=B#), D#, F#, C natural is the tension note; it aches to resolve up to C#
F#m
F#, A, C#, A is the warm middle note; C# connects back to the key center
B
B, D#, F#, D# is bright against the minor surroundings
E
E, G#, B, stable and open; G# is shared with G#7, smooth voice leading
E7
E, G#, B, D, the D is the dissonant pull; resolve it down to C# on the next chord

Chord Reference

Intro / Chorus / Instrumental Chords (D minor world)

Chord Notes Function in Dm
Dm D – F – A i (tonic minor)
Bb Bb – D – F VI (major subtonic)
C C – E – G VII (natural major)
A A – C# – E V (borrowed from harm. minor)

The Bb → C → Dm motion is the engine of the riff. A major is a surprise, C# is not in D natural minor, but D harmonic minor supplies it. That C# is what gives the A chord its urgency.

Verse / Bridge Chords (C# minor world)

Chord Notes Function in C#m
C#m7 C# – E – G# – B i7 (tonic minor seventh)
G#7 G# – C – D# – F# V7 (dominant seventh; C = B#, raised 7th)
F#m F# – A – C# iv (subdominant minor)
B B – D# – F# VII (natural major)
E E – G# – B III (relative major)
E7 E – G# – B – D III7 (dominant of VI?)
D D – F# – A bII (Neapolitan-adjacent, passing)
A A – C# – E VI (major submediant)

Note: G#7's "C" is enharmonically B#, the raised 7th in C# harmonic minor. This is what gives the G#7 its classical dominant-seventh pull back to C#m.

Advanced Inversions

Inversions let you voice-lead smoothly between chords instead of jumping around the keyboard.

Intro / Chorus Chord Inversions

Chord Inversion Notes (low → high) When to Use
Dm Root D – F – A Default; strong landing on the i chord
Dm/F 1st inversion F – A – D Coming from Bb; F in bass is a smooth step
Dm/A 2nd inversion A – D – F Pedal-like; A in bass anticipates A chord
Bb Root Bb – D – F Default; Bb is the big resonant VI
Bb/D 1st inversion D – F – Bb Walking bass D → E(b) → F; keeps motion
Bb/F 2nd inversion F – Bb – D Floaty; connects C/G to Dm via stepwise F
C Root C – E – G Default for VII
C/E 1st inversion E – G – C Bass rises E → F → (Dm root); elegant
C/G 2nd inversion G – C – E Pedal G under the VII chord
A Root A – C# – E Full V resolution to Dm
A/C# 1st inversion C# – E – A C# in bass, dramatic chromatic descent to D
A/E 2nd inversion E – A – C# Lighter dominant; less resolved feel

Verse / Bridge Chord Inversions

Chord Inversion Notes (low → high) When to Use
C#m7 Root C# – E – G# – B Default; rich tonic
C#m7/E 1st inversion E – G# – B – C# E in bass connects to E major chord naturally
C#m7/G# 2nd inversion G# – B – C# – E G# bass echoes G#7; nice pivot point
C#m7/B 3rd inversion B – C# – E – G# B in bass walks down toward A or G#
G#7 Root G# – C – D# – F# Strong dominant pull to C#m

G#7/D# 2nd inversion D# – F# – G# – C Mid-range color; less resolved

F#m Root F# – A – C# Default; solid subdominant
F#m/A 1st inversion A – C# – F# A in bass, smooth step from A major chord
F#m/C# 2nd inversion C# – F# – A C# connects back to C#m; cyclical feel
B Root B – D# – F# Default VII chord
B/D# 1st inversion D# – F# – B D# in bass, ascending line from D
B/F# 2nd inversion F# – B – D# Pedal-like; F# shared with F#m
E Root E – G# – B Default III chord
E/G# 1st inversion G# – B – E G# bass connects to G#7 smoothly
E/B 2nd inversion B – E – G# B in bass; voice-leads into B major chord
E7 Root E – G# – B – D Default; the D adds tension
E7/G# 1st inversion G# – B – D – E Smooth from E root; inner voice motion
E7/B 2nd inversion B – D – E – G# Walking bass feel
E7/D 3rd inversion D – E – G# – B D in bass resolves down to C#; voice-leading gem

Voice-leading tip for the verse:

The bass line C# → G# → F# → B → E → E → C# forms a powerful descending pattern. Play inversions to keep bass motion as stepwise as possible rather than jumping in octaves.

Performance Notes

  • Intro riff: The Bb → C → Dm pattern is everything. LH holds roots, RH plays the melody line over the top. Keep the C chord crisp, don't blur it into Dm.
  • The A major chord: That C# is a surprise. Let it ring. It resolves the tension built by Bb and C before landing on Dm.
  • Verse dynamic: The C#m7 verse should feel tighter and more urgent than the open D minor sections. Pedal use here is minimal.
  • G#7 → C#m7: Classic V7 → i resolution. The C natural inside G#7 is the knife-edge. Let it resolve cleanly to C#.
  • The chorus pivot: Going from A major (in C# minor, that's the VI) back to Dm for the chorus is a key change up a semitone. The A chord is the bridge between both keys, it's the V of Dm and the VI of C#m.
  • Piano coda / Outro: The Dm riff slows and opens up under the final chorus repeats. Let Bb breathe and ring. The last line strips back to a bare C → Dm, hold that final Dm and let it decay.
