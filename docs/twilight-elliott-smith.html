Introduction

Oh, wow. What a hauntingly beautiful song from Elliott Smith's final studio album, From a Basement on the Hill (2004). You can listen to the song here.

The cheat sheet here provides some simplified chords for playing this song on the piano. It stays in a fairly C major scale, but with a sad A minor feel.

Key & theory

  • Key: C major / A minor
  • Feel: Bittersweet, suspended, introspective
  • The Fmaj7/C is the emotional anchor, that E on top gives it an airy, unresolved quality
  • D7 is the tension chord; the F# pulls hard toward resolution
  • C/G acts as a passing chord — the G bass creates forward motion without a full cadence

Scales & Improvisation

Primary Scales

Notes to Emphasize Over Each Chord

Fmaj7/C
F, A, C, E — the E on top is the most distinctive note; lean on it
Am
A, C, E — home base; anything in the minor scale sounds right
G
G, B, D — B natural is outside the minor pentatonic but essential here
F
F, A, C — warm and grounded; A and C are safe landing notes
C/G
C, E, G — E sounds bright; land on G for a gentle passing feel
D7
D, A — safe anchors; F# (the raised 7th) adds tension — use sparingly

The D7 Exception

D7 contains F#, which sits outside A natural minor. Two options:

  1. Avoid F# — stay on D, A, C. It still works.
  2. Use F# as a brief passing note rising to G — it pulls hard upward and resolves beautifully.

Improvisation Tips for Beginners

  • Start with A minor pentatonic (A C D E G) — only 5 notes, all safe, all sound good
  • Play slowly and leave gaps — Elliott Smith's melodic style is sparse, not busy
  • Land on chord tones at the end of phrases (the notes of whichever chord is playing)
  • The note E is especially resonant over Fmaj7/C — return to it often
  • Avoid rushing to fill silence; the rests are part of the melody

Style Notes

Elliott Smith Piano Approach

  • Sparse > dense. Leave space.
  • Let bass notes sustain while inner voices move
  • Avoid heavy attack — aim for a soft, almost hesitant touch
  • The melody sits on top; chord tones underneath, not competing

Voicing Tips

  • Drop the 5th from most chords — it still sounds full on piano
  • Fmaj7/C: left hand C alone, right hand F-A-E is especially open and lovely
  • Keep right hand voicings in the middle register (not too high)

Dynamics

  • Stay mostly in the p to mp range
  • Swell slightly on the G chord resolutions
  • D7 can have a little more weight to emphasize the tension
