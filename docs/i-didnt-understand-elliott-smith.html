Introduction

"I Didn't Understand" closes Elliott Smith's 1998 album XO, and it is unlike anything else in his catalog: it is entirely a cappella. No guitar, no piano, no drums, nothing, just Elliott's voice multitracked into a small choir, harmonizing with himself. The Beach-Boys-by-way-of-the-Beatles vocal stack carries the entire harmony.

That makes a piano "cheat sheet" a slightly strange object. You are not copying an instrumental part, there isn't one. You are reducing a vocal arrangement to the keyboard, the way you'd play a hymn or a choral score at the piano. Treat every chord as a clump of singers, not a strummed shape. The reward is that the harmony is gorgeous and the voice leading is the whole point.

Capo & Transposition (read this first)

The guitar chart this is based on is labeled "Capo 3" with chords G#m, B, E, F#, C#m. There is no "transpose to X" note (compare the Crawlspace sheet, where there was), and the chords are barre-heavy, not easy open shapes. That tells you the capo here is not for easy fingering, it is to lift a guitar transcription up to Elliott's a cappella vocal pitch.

Under the standard chord-sheet convention, the chords printed are the shapes fingered above the capo, and the music actually sounds three semitones higher. A piano has no capo, so it plays the real, sounding pitches.

⚠️ Do not play G#m–B–E–F#–C#m on the piano. Those are the capo'd guitar shapes. The song actually sounds in B minor, and your five chords are Bm, D, G, A, Em. Everything in the rest of this sheet is in that real, sounding key.

Key & Theory

The song lives in B natural minor (Aeolian), the relative minor of D major. Every chord is diatonic to B natural minor, and, crucially, there is no F# major (no raised leading tone, no dominant V). That missing leading tone is why the song feels permanently unresolved and sad rather than dramatic; it never gets the strong V→i pull that would let it land with finality.

Chord Role Comment
Bm i tonic, the home the song keeps circling
D III the relative major, a brief warmth
G VI the soft, sighing lift
A VII subtonic (not a leading tone), pushes to D or Bm
Em iv minor subdominant, the ache in the chorus

The verse runs Bm → G → A (i → VI → VII), the quintessential Aeolian descent-feel loop, then settles with Bm → D → G (i → III → VI) under "tonight, tomorrow and every day." The chorus opens A → D (VII → III) and resolves through Em → D → Em → Bm (iv → III → iv → i), a gentle minor plagal motion that finally touches home on Bm at "What a fucking joke" / "I didn't understand."

Note that the chord built on scale degree 5 is F#m, not F# major. The song uses neither, the dominant function is simply absent. This is pure Aeolian, and it is a big part of the Elliott Smith sound.

Tips

  • Voice these like a choir, not a band. Close, mid-register, four-ish voices, smooth. This is a vocal arrangement; play it as one
  • Bm → G shares two common tones. Bm (B D F#) → G (G B D): keep B and D exactly where they are; only F# steps down to G. The change should barely move
  • Bm → D also shares two common tones (the relative-major move). Bm (B D F#) → D (D F# A): keep D and F#; only B steps down to A
  • Em → D is a clean parallel slide: every voice drops a whole step, E→D, G→F#, B→A. Let the whole hand fall together, smooth and resigned
  • G → A (VI → VII) has no common tones, every voice steps up a whole step together. This is the one place the harmony actively rises; let it feel like a small, hopeful lift before it sinks back
  • Em → Bm (iv → i) shares B. This is the song's resolution, soft, not triumphant. Don't punch it; let it settle

Inversions for Voice Leading

The whole piece is small, smooth motion between five chords. Inversions keep the choir tight and the common tones ringing.

Chord Position Notes (low→high) When to Use
Bm Root B, D, F# Default tonic
Bm/D 1st inv. D, F#, B D in bass ties Bm into D (III)
Bm/F# 2nd inv. F#, B, D F# pedal; quiet, hovering tonic
D Root D, F#, A Default III
D/F# 1st inv. F#, A, D F# held from Bm; only one voice moves
D/A 2nd inv. A, D, F# A pedal, ties A (VII) into D
G Root G, B, D Default VI
G/B 1st inv. B, D, G B & D held from Bm; barely-moving change
G/D 2nd inv. D, G, B D pedal between D and G
A Root A, C#, E Default VII
A/C# 1st inv. C#, E, A C# in bass walks up toward D
Em Root E, G, B Default iv
Em/G 1st inv. G, B, E G & B held from G chord
Em/B 2nd inv. B, E, G B pedal; ties Em into Bm resolution

Voice-leading recipe for the verse:

Play Bm in root position, then G in first inversion (B, D, G). The right hand keeps B and D dead still and only lifts F# → G. The choir scarcely breathes. Then A in root position for the lift, and back to Bm. Keep it low and close, this is murmured, not projected.

For the chorus resolution, Em → Bm: play Em in root position and Bm in second inversion (F#, B, D), or simply keep B as a common tone between them. The arrival on Bm should feel like a held breath let out, not a cadence slammed shut.

Performance Notes

  • This song has no instruments. Everything below is your reconstruction of the vocal harmony. Aim for a choral, sustained sound, not a comping pattern
  • Tempo: slow and rubato. There is no drum, no groove, nothing keeping strict time on the record, the phrases breathe with the lyric. Roughly a slow ~80 BPM feel, but bend it freely. Follow the words, not a metronome
  • Touch: legato and soft throughout. Connect chords by hand as much as by pedal. Think of each chord as voices sustaining and resolving into the next
  • Pedal: gentle, and change cleanly on every chord so the choir stays clear. No wash. When in doubt, under-pedal and connect with the fingers instead
  • Dynamics: hushed. This is a confession at the end of the album, not a chorus to belt. The loudest the harmony ever gets is the resigned "What a fucking joke," and even that is more bitter than loud
  • The verse (Bm G A / Bm D G): keep it murmured and close. The Bm → G → A loop is the heart of the song; let it cycle without drawing attention to the chord changes
  • The chorus (A D / Em D Em Bm): the move into Em (iv) is the emotional peak, the minor subdominant is where the ache lives. Lean on it slightly, then let Em → Bm sink home. Do not brighten it; the resolution is sad, not relieved
  • The repeated tags ("What a fucking joke", "I didn't understand"): each repetition is Em → D then finally Em → Bm. Play them progressively quieter, the song doesn't build, it deflates. End on Bm with no flourish; let it simply stop, the way the record's voices simply stop
  • Optional, true to the original: instead of solid triads, arpeggiate or stagger the chord tones so individual "voices" enter one at a time, the way the vocal stack does. This is closer to the record than block chords, and it suits the rubato feel
