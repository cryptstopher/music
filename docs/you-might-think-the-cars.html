Introduction

"You Might Think" is the opening single from The Cars' 1984 album Heartbeat City, the record that completed the band's pivot from skinny-tie new wave to glossy, MTV-ready synth pop. Ric Ocasek wrote it; Mutt Lange co-produced it; and the cartoon-collage video (with Ocasek pestering a woman from inside a toothpaste tube, a fly, a King Kong arm) won the very first MTV Video Music Award for Video of the Year. The song is a love-as-mild-stalking pep talk delivered with a grin: "you might think I'm crazy / but all I want is you."

Harmonically the song is almost stubbornly plain. Four diatonic chords do nearly everything: D, G, A, Bm. The single move that lifts it out of campfire territory is the chorus turnaround Bm → Bm/C# → D, where the bass walks up chromatically B → C# → D under what is essentially the same vi chord. Get that walk-up tight and the song sings; miss it and the chorus goes flat.

Key & Theory

The song lives in D major. Every chord is diatonic except for the bass note of Bm/C#, which is a passing tone, not a real chromatic chord.

Chord Role Comment
D I tonic, home of the verse, chorus, and outro
G IV the lift, paired with D throughout
A V the dominant, drives back into D
Bm vi the relative minor, the pre-chorus's home
Bm/C# vi (inv.) passing inversion; C# in bass walks Bm → D

The verse is a straight D → G → A → D loop, I IV V I, the most generic progression in pop music, used here without apology. The pre-chorus shifts to Bm → A → G, vi V IV, a descending walk that pulls the energy down so the chorus can pop back up. The chorus opens with the same D → G loop but ends each line with Bm → Bm/C# → D, that chromatic bass walk-up which is the song's harmonic signature.

The bridge stretches Bm → G → A into longer phrases and then strips down to two N.C. (no chord) hits on G and A under "but you kept it going till the sun fell down." This is the only place in the song where the harmony breathes, every other section is wall-to-wall chords.

Four chords, the I, IV, V, and vi, do all the work. This is the "four chords" pop set sitting on D. Notice that C# never appears as a chord, only as a bass note inside Bm/C#. That single passing tone is the closest the song gets to chromaticism.

Tips

  • LH plays the root in the bass; RH holds the triad. This is a triad song, no sevenths, no extensions. The shimmer comes from the rhythm and the bass walk, not from clever voicings
  • D → G shares one common tone, D. D (D F# A) → G (G B D): keep D as the top note; F# and A drop a step to G and B. Right hand barely moves
  • G → A shares no common tones; every voice steps up by a whole step. Move the whole hand together. This is a "shove" change, lean into it
  • A → D (V → I) is the cadence. A (A C# E) → D (D F# A): C# rises to D, E rises to F#, A holds. Smooth and final
  • Bm → A → G (the pre-chorus walk-down) has stepwise bass motion B → A → G. Let the bass do the work and keep the right-hand triads in close position. The descending bass is the hook of the pre-chorus
  • Bm → Bm/C# → D is the song's signature move. See below

Bm/C# (the chorus walk-up)

The Bm/C# notation looks fancy but the right hand does not change. Only the bass note moves. 

Bm:     LH = B    RH = B, D, F#
Bm/C#:  LH = C#   RH = B, D, F#   (same RH; bass walks up)
D:      LH = D    RH = D, F#, A   (RH shifts up one step)

The bass line B → C# → D is a smooth chromatic ascent that pulls the ear into the tonic D. The C# is the leading tone of D major, so the listener hears it as "almost home, then home." Without that C# the move from Bm straight to D feels lumpy; with it, the chorus glides. 

LH:   B   →   C#   →   D
RH:  B D F#  B D F#  D F# A

If you skip the inversion and just play Bm → D, the song still works but loses its signature lift. This is the one piece of the arrangement worth not skipping.

Inversions for Voice Leading

The chorus moves D, G, A, Bm, Bm/C#, D in tight succession. Inversions keep the right hand from sprinting up and down the keyboard.

Chord Position Notes (low→high) When to Use
D Root D, F#, A Default tonic
D/F# 1st inv. F#, A, D F# in bass walks toward G
D/A 2nd inv. A, D, F# A pedal between A chord and D chord
G Root G, B, D Default IV
G/B 1st inv. B, D, G B in bass; ties G to Bm
G/D 2nd inv. D, G, B D pedal from D chord
A Root A, C#, E Default V
A/C# 1st inv. C#, E, A C# in bass walks toward D
A/E 2nd inv. E, A, C# E pedal; rare here
Bm Root B, D, F# Default vi
Bm/D 1st inv. D, F#, B D pedal; ties Bm into D chord
Bm/F# 2nd inv. F#, B, D F# in bass; rare here
Bm/C# (passing) C# bass, B D F# above the chorus walk-up; not a true inversion

Voice-leading recipe for the verse:

Play D in root position, then G in second inversion (D, G, B). The right hand keeps D dead still on top and only nudges F# → G and A → B. The bass moves D → G, a perfect fourth, but the right hand barely flinches. This keeps the verse texture even and lets the vocal sit on top.

For the chorus walk-up, the cleanest path is: 

Bm    (root)      LH B   RH B D F#
Bm/C# (passing)   LH C#  RH B D F#   (RH unchanged, bass up a half-step)
D     (root)      LH D   RH D F# A   (RH up one step)

Bass walks B → C# → D, three half-steps that pull straight into the tonic.

Performance Notes

  • Tempo: bright and bouncy, around 134 BPM. This is a synth-pop pep song, not a ballad. Keep the pulse even and the energy up
  • Touch: percussive on the verses (think eighth-note chord stabs, like a Linn drum's snare), more sustained on the chorus. The original is arpeggiated synth; on piano you can either stab the chords or arpeggiate them, both work, pick one and stay with it
  • Pedal: light. This is a clean, punchy track, not a wash. Change the pedal on every chord and keep it shallow. On the Bm → Bm/C# → D walk, consider lifting the pedal entirely so the bass line stays articulate
  • Dynamics: verses are medium, pre-chorus pulls back slightly (the descending Bm A G walk should feel like a coiling spring), chorus opens up to full volume. The bridge is the only quiet moment, especially the two N.C. bars
  • The intro (D G A D / D G A Bm / G A D D): set the tempo and the texture. Play it like a drum fill, all rhythm, no embellishment. Land hard on the final D so the verse can start
  • The verse (D G A D): keep it simple and even. Every measure gets the same I IV V I. The drummer's job, not the harmonist's
  • The pre-chorus (Bm A G): bass walks down B → A → G. Let that descending line be the hook; keep the right-hand triads close and unobtrusive. This is the song coiling before the chorus springs
  • The chorus (D G / A Bm Bm/C# / D G / A D G A): the Bm Bm/C# D walk is the moment. Lock the bass: B (downbeat) → C# (and-of-beat) → D (next downbeat). The right hand can stay still on B D F# through the Bm and Bm/C#, then shift to D F# A on the D. Practice this slowly until the chromatic bass feels automatic
  • The bridge (Bm G A Bm / G A Bm G A G): same chord palette, stretched into longer phrases. Then the G N.C. and A N.C. under "but you kept it going till the sun fell down" are the only stop-time moments in the song. Hit the chord, kill it, let the vocal sit in silence. Then hit the next chord
  • The instrumental (D G A D / D G A Bm / G A D): same as the intro. In the original this is a guitar solo over the verse changes; on solo piano, improvise a melody in D major pentatonic (D E F# A B) over the changes, or play the chords as bright eighth-note stabs and let the rhythm carry it
  • The final chorus extends the standard chorus with an extra G A Bm / G A D tag at the end on "all I want is you / all I want is you." Ride the energy out at full volume and land hard on the final D. Like "Crawlspace," this song stops, it does not dissolve
